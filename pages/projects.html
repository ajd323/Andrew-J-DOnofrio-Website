Consumer Products

IoT · LoRaWAN · Product Development · User Research

Red Newt Racing GPS Baton Tracker

Led mechanical design for a reusable IoT smart baton for the Seneca 7, a 77.7-mile relay race around Seneca Lake (2,000+ participants, 300+ teams). The device transmits GPS coordinates and baton handoff events every 30 seconds via LoRaWAN to a custom Streamlit web interface accessible by race officials in real time.

Progressed through three enclosure iterations incorporating ergonomic ridges, a carabiner loop, weather-protected ports, and a woven rear strap. Conducted a user study with 45 runners to inform form factor decisions and deployed the system at the Seneca 7 race and the Belle Sherman 5K.

Medical Device

Sports Medicine · Diagnostic Device · FDA 510(k) · VentureWell

Axio Recovery

Led project management and systems engineering for a continuous athletic monitor estimating valgus elbow torque in MLB players during UCL surgery recovery. Translated the Hills Musculoskeletal model into a full-scale Python-based processing pipeline, synthesizing sEMG and IMU signals and validating outputs with OptiTrack motion capture data.

Iterated to V2.0 achieving 2.58% peak torque error against OptiTrack ground truth, a 15x improvement over competing consumer devices. Completed a full DEBUT VentureWell Phase 2B feasibility report including regulatory 510(k), reimbursement, and commercialization analysis.

sEMG / IMU Hill's Musculoskeletal Model Python FDA 510(k) VentureWell / DEBUT

Assistive Technology · Parkinson's Disease · Award Winner

Steady Stride: The Tremor-Resistant Cane

Led the full product development lifecycle and clinical testing for an assistive walking cane designed to resist Parkinson's disease hand tremors. Managed a team of 10+ student engineers through rapid prototyping and MATLAB simulations as R&D Analyst for Cornell DEBUT.

Winner of the Medtronic BMES 2024 Student Design Competition (Mechanical Track) and $2,500 for commercialization. Conducted IRB-approved trials with Parkinson's patients at the SUNY Cortland Biomechanics Laboratory, analyzing over 1 million data points to achieve approximately 12% reduction in tremor amplitude.

CACOM: Carotid Artery Cardiac Output Monitor

Designed and tested the electromechanical components for a non-invasive, continuous blood flow monitoring device for individuals with low cardiovascular output. Designed a custom 2-layer PCB interfacing with pressure and ultrasonic sensors, and formulated carotid artery FEA for applanation tonometry to estimate arterial deformation with less than 5% error.
